    Methodology (2025)¶

    • Survey: "a global survey conducted between June 13 and July 21, 2025" (2025 report, p. 4, Key findings).
    • Sample: "This year, a total of 4,867 respondents answered our survey" (p. 103, Demographics and firmographics). In the executive summary: "more than 100 hours of qualitative data and survey responses from nearly 5,000 technology professionals" (p. 3).
    • Interviews: "In total, we interviewed 78 participants", continuously "from July 2024–July 2025" (p. 115, Methodology).
    • Survey in four randomly assigned streams: "AI", "Platform engineering", "Sociocognitive aspects", "AI capabilities" (p. 114). Each respondent saw only one stream, so not every question has all 4,867.
    • The effects are standardized Bayesian estimates "with 89% credible intervals" (Figure 1, p. 4; Figure 28, p. 38): the 2025 report gives direction and standardized size, not percentages like the 2024 one.
    • Instability in 2025 = "Change fail rate" + "Rework rate"; throughput = "Lead time for changes", "Deployment frequency", "Failed deployment recovery time" (p. 13, Software delivery performance factors).

    Methodology (2024)¶

    • "This year, nearly 3,000 working professionals from a variety of industries around the world shared their experiences" (2024 report, p. 91, Demographics and firmographics). History: "roughly 39,000 professionals" over the course of the program.

    AI, throughput and instability (2025) — the slide's primary source¶

    • "AI adoption now improves software delivery throughput, a key shift from last year. However, it still increases delivery instability. This suggests that while teams are adapting for speed, their underlying systems have not yet evolved to safely manage AI-accelerated development." (p. 4, Key findings).
    • The person with higher AI adoption reports "Higher levels of software delivery instability" and "Higher levels of software delivery throughput" (p. 38, The results this year).
    • "AI's relationship with software delivery throughput has turned from negative to positive" (p. 42, Changes in last year's patterns suggest adaptation).
    • "it continues its detrimental relationship with software delivery stability" (p. 43, Conclusion).
    • Blog: "AI accelerates software development, but that acceleration can expose weaknesses downstream. Without robust control systems, like strong automated testing, mature version control practices, and fast feedback loops, an increase in change volume leads to instability." (Google Cloud Blog, 2025-09-23, section "Key findings from the 2025 report").
    • dora.dev (2026-03-10): "higher AI adoption is associated with an increase in both software delivery throughput and software delivery instability"; proposed mechanism: "the time saved in creation is frequently re-allocated to auditing and verification".

    AI and delivery in 2024 (literal numbers)¶

    • Section "AI is hurting delivery performance", Figure 10 (2024 report, p. 39): "If AI adoption increases by 25%…" → "Delivery throughput -1.5%", "Delivery stability -7.2%".
    • "We see that the effect on delivery throughput is small, but likely negative (an estimated 1.5% reduction for every 25% increase in AI adoption). The negative impact on delivery stability is larger (an estimated 7.2% reduction for every 25% increase in AI adoption)." (p. 40).
    • DORA's hypothesis: "since AI allows respondents to produce a much greater amount of code in the same amount of time, it is possible, even likely, that changelists are growing in size. DORA has consistently shown that larger changes are slower and more prone to creating instability." (p. 40).

    AI as amplifier¶

    • "AI's primary role in software development is that of an amplifier. It magnifies the strengths of high-performing organizations and the dysfunctions of struggling ones." (2025 report, p. 3, Key takeaway).
    • "Without this foundation, AI creates localized pockets of productivity that are often lost to downstream chaos." (p. 3).
    • Blog: "AI doesn't fix a team; it amplifies what's already there." (Google Cloud Blog, section "AI, the great amplifier").
    • Adoption: "The majority of survey respondents (90%) use AI as part of their work and believe (more than 80%) it has increased their productivity. Yet a notable portion (30%) currently report little to no trust in the code generated by AI" (p. 4).

    DORA AI Capabilities Model (7 capabilities)¶

    Literal list (AI Capabilities Model, p. 4, Executive summary):

    1. "Clear and communicated AI stance" — "Ambiguity creates risk. A clear policy provides the psychological safety needed for effective experimentation."
    2. "Healthy data ecosystems" — "The benefits of AI are significantly amplified by high-quality, accessible, and unified internal data."
    3. "AI-accessible internal data" — "Connecting AI to your internal documentation and codebases moves it from a generic assistant to a specialized expert."
    4. "Strong version control practices" — "As AI increases the velocity of change, version control becomes the critical safety net that enables confident experimentation."
    5. "Working in small batches" — "This discipline counteracts the risk of AI generating large, unstable changes, ensuring that speed translates to better product performance."
    6. "User-centric focus" — "A focus on user needs is essential to ensure that AI-accelerated teams are moving quickly in the right direction."
    7. "Quality internal platforms" — "A platform provides the automated, secure pathways that allow AI's benefits to scale across the organization."

    Platform as a precondition for AI (2025)¶

    • "90% of organizations have adopted platform engineering, making a high-quality internal platform the essential foundation for AI success." (2025 report, p. 4).
    • "Our data shows that 90% of organizations have adopted at least one platform, with 29% of organizations now using a multi-platform environment." and "76% of organizations have at least one dedicated platform team" (p. 67, The platform landscape).
    • "AI adoption has a negligible effect on organizational performance when platform quality is low, but when platform quality is high, the effect is strong and positive." (p. 71, The strategic imperative; Figure 49).
    • "With a high degree of certainty, we found that AI adoption's impacts depend on organizations having quality internal platforms." (p. 62, Quality internal platforms). But also: "respondents experience more friction in organizations with quality internal platforms" (p. 62).
    • Platform quality = "a single score, indicating how many of 12 characteristics a respondent indicates their internal platforms have" (p. 62).
    • "An investment in AI without a corresponding investment in high-quality platforms is unlikely to yield significant returns at the organizational level." (p. 72).
    • Individual productivity gains "are often lost to downstream disorder, swallowed by bottlenecks in testing, security reviews, and complex deployment processes." (AI Capabilities Model, p. 59, Quality internal platforms).
    • The platform also increases instability: "a better platform is associated with a small but credible increase in software delivery instability, meaning a higher change failure rate and increased rework." DORA's reading: "a manageable trade-off for the significant gains in performance" (2025 report, p. 70).
    • "Experience gap": technical capabilities (reliability, security) are rated well; "acting on feedback" and "how well tasks are automated" lag behind (p. 67). "the capability most correlated with a positive user experience is providing clear feedback to tasks" (p. 69).
    • Named antipatterns: "Build it and they will come", "The ticket-ops trap", "The ivory tower platform" (p. 68).

    Platform engineering in 2024 (literal numbers)¶

    • "we found that 89% of respondents are using an internal developer platform" (2024 report, p. 50).
    • "Internal developer platform users had 8% higher levels of individual productivity and 10% higher levels of team performance. Additionally, an organization's software delivery and operations performance increases 6% when using a platform. However, these gains do not come without some drawbacks. Throughput and change stability saw decreases of 8% and 14%, respectively" (p. 49).
    • Developer independence: "a 5% improvement in productivity when users of the platform are able to complete their tasks without involving an enabling team" (p. 51). Mandatory platform: "there was a 6% decrease in throughput" for those who must "exclusively use the platform" (p. 53).

    Value stream management (2025)¶

    • "Value stream management (VSM), the practice of visualizing, analyzing, and improving the flow of work from idea to customer, acts as a force multiplier for AI, ensuring that local productivity gains translate into measurable improvements in team and product performance." (2025 report, p. 4).
    • "Without VSM, AI risks creating localized efficiencies that are simply absorbed by downstream bottlenecks, delivering no real value to the organization as a whole." (p. 77). Stated findings: "VSM drives team performance", "VSM leads to more valuable work", "VSM improves product performance" (p. 77).
    • Note: p. 77 phrases "VSM moderates the relationship between AI adoption and organizational performance" as a hypothesis ("We hypothesize"); the executive summary (p. 4-5) treats it as a finding.

    Not verified¶

    • 2026 edition of State of AI-assisted Software Development: not found; dora.dev/research/ lists up to 2025 (read on 2026-10-07).
    • "ROI of AI-assisted Software Development" (2026): read in full on 2026-10-07 — see dora-roi-ai.md. The numbers cited by third parties (J-curve, CFR from 5% to 6%, 39% ROI) are there, but they are illustrative assumptions of the calculator, not findings.
    • The size of the AI effect on instability in 2025 exists only in a chart (Figure 28, standardized scale ~-0.05 to 0.20); there is no literal number in the text — do not cite a percentage for 2025.
    • List of the 12 characteristics of a quality platform (Appendix of the 2025 report): not read.

    • Platform-as-a-precondition-for-AI slides (platform-readiness, rebuild-platform): p. 71 ("negligible effect… when platform quality is low"), p. 72 ("An investment in AI without a corresponding investment in high-quality platforms…"), 90% / 76% / 29% (p. 67) and the 7th capability of the AI Capabilities Model.
