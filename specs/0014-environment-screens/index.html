    0014. The Environments in the portal: each Environment's health and its Workloads live, and a search by name in the lists¶

    Status: Approved by the owner (2026-11-26), on the prototype; the calls below were accepted with it

    From the owner's session of 2026-11-26. The owner shapes it on a clickable prototype kept next to this file: 0014-environment-screens/prototype.html (invented data, simulated live updates), following spec 0013 (the Nereids' screens), which it reuses. Raw data in the session's Pontos log.

    Intent¶

    The owner's hypothesis: seeing the applications that run in an Environment, each with its own metrics in the style of the Nereids' screens (spec 0013), next to the health of the Environment itself, helps developers analyse the health of their applications. The question the screens answer first is a developer's: "is the problem in my application or in the environment?". The owner remembered how New Relic shows a cluster's health.

    The owner also misses a search by name in the Nereids' list today; since this work changes the portal anyway, the same search enters both screens.

    The screens follow Material Design 3, as the portal does since #1838, and are approved on the prototype before any implementation.

    Decisions (owner, 2026-11-26)¶

    • The developer first. The Environment's health shows the few signals that tell a developer whether the environment is the cause (nodes, pressure, pending pods, a pending upgrade). The platform operator's denser view (capacity, nodes in detail, the upgrade plan of #353) comes later, as a tab of the same screen.
    • One screen per Environment (approach A below): the health card on top, the Workloads below in a list or a grid, problems first; a Workload opens its own detail screen, shaped like a Nereid's (spec 0013).
    • A search by name, in this journey, in both the Environments' screens and the Nereids' home. A magnifier on the row of the filter chips; a tap expands it into a field that filters in real time as one types; it contracts when one leaves it.

    Approaches considered¶

    How the Environment's screens are arranged.

    • A. One screen per Environment, the health card on top and the Workloads below: chosen. "My app or the environment?" is answered in one glance, and the screen reuses the components of the Nereids' home (summary card, chips, list and grid). The card can grow into the operator's tab later without redoing the screen.
    • B. An Environment with two tabs, Workloads and Health: more room for the health and an obvious place for the operator's tab, but comparing the application with the environment means switching tabs.
    • C. The Workloads at the top level ("my apps"), the Environment a chip, its health a banner on the Workload's detail: the shortest path to one's own application, but the Environment loses its own screen, and the owner's idea starts from it.

    The screens¶

    The prototype is the reference for the look; the words below are the rules. Measured at 340 and 360 px.

    Environments (a new destination of the bottom navigation)¶

    ┌ Environments                             ┐
│ shop · portal.example.com                │
│ ┌──────────────────────────────────────┐ │
│ │ ● 2 need attention              ⟳   │ │
│ │   3 environments · 11 workloads      │ │
│ └──────────────────────────────────────┘ │
│ (All)(Attention)(Ready)            [🔍] │  ← chips, search
│ Needs attention · 2                      │
│ ▣ shop-dev · project/shop        Ready   │
│   7 workloads · 2 need attention         │
│   nodes 3/3  cpu 41%  mem 63%  ↑ 1.32    │
│ ▣ pr-2291 · project/shop    Progressing  │
│   2 workloads · 1 need attention ·       │
│   expires in 2d                          │
│─────────────────────────────────────────│
│  Status    Environments    Approvals     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
    • The Environments a person sees are those whose spec.owner is a Project or Organization the person takes part in (ADR 0015, the tenancy tree), as the Nereus API already authorizes reading them.
    • An Environment needs attention when its own health does or when any of its Workloads does; both sections, "Needs attention" and "All good", as on the Nereids' home.
    • Each row: the name, the owner, the state, the Workloads' count and how many need attention, the expiry when it has one (status.expiresAt, ADR 0026), then one mono line: nodes ready, CPU and memory requested of capacity, and ↑ <version> when an upgrade is available.
    • No view toggle here: there are few Environments; the toggle lives where many items are (Workloads, Nereids).

    • The health card says in one line whether the environment is healthy or under pressure, and why when it is not, then four cells: nodes ready over total, pods pending, CPU requested of capacity and memory requested of capacity, each of the last two with a meter (amber above 70%, red above 85%). Two states (owner, 2026-11-27, #2306): under pressure when a pod is pending, a node does not answer or a meter passes 70%, else healthy; missing data shows as no data, never a zero.
    • A pending upgrade shows as a tonal amber line: the version available and until when the current one is supported. It is information for the developer, never an action here; the upgrade itself is #353's.
    • The expiry, for an Environment that has one, as a neutral line: "Expires in 2d, with its workloads".
    • The Workloads in a list or a grid, problems first, the same as the Nereids' home of spec 0013: the list row carries the reason of a problem, the RED line (requests per second, the 5xx ratio, p95) and a sparkline of the last hour; the grid card one big number and a sparkline. A Workload with no route at the edge shows "no route at the edge" in the list and its CPU in the grid, and its sparkline is its CPU.
    • The view toggle is one icon button showing the other view (M3 icon button), kept in the browser per screen kind.

    • The same layout as a Nereid's detail (spec 0013): the range as a segmented button (5m, 1h default, 24h, 7d), the four RED signals, then the pods (CPU and memory against request and limit, restarts in 24h, one line per pod with its node, age, restarts and the last restart's reason).
    • RED comes from the edge, the requests the edge Reef routes to the Workload: rate, the 5xx ratio and p95. Saturation is the Workload's CPU of its limit. A Workload with no route at the edge (a worker, a cache) shows its Saturation and its pods, and the Requests card says why it has no traffic numbers.
    • When the Environment itself is under pressure, a line under the Workload's reason says so, so the developer sees the cause without going back.

    The search, on the row of the chips¶

    closed            (All)(Attention)(Ready)        [🔍] [▦]
open, typing      [🔍 chec___________________ ✕] [▦]
                  1 of 7 workloads · "chec"         Clear
                  ⊕ checkout · 1/2 pods    Degraded
left, with text   [🔍 chec___________________ ✕] [▦]
left, empty       (All)(Attention)(Ready)        [🔍] [▦]
    • Where: an outlined round icon button with a magnifier on the row of the filter chips, before the view toggle, on the Environments, on one Environment's Workloads and on the Nereids' home.
    • Opening: a tap expands it into a filled field across the row (the chips hide while it is open), with the keyboard up and the cursor in it; the expansion is animated, and instant under prefers-reduced-motion.
    • Filtering: in real time on every keystroke, case-insensitive, a substring of the name: the Environment's name; the Workload's name; the Reef's or the Nereid's name on the Nereids' home. The match is underlined in the row. The filter chips still apply (both together), and the sections keep their counts.
    • The line under the row says how many match out of how many and repeats the text, with a "Clear" button. With nothing matching, the list says so and offers "Clear the search".
    • Leaving: the field contracts back to the magnifier when one leaves it empty (tap elsewhere, Enter, the keyboard's search key). With text in it, it stays open, so what filters the list is never hidden; ✕ clears and keeps the cursor in it; Escape clears and contracts.
    • Kept: the text lives in the screen's state only; leaving the screen or switching tabs clears it. It is not written to the URL nor to localStorage.

    Where the data comes from¶

    • Through the Nereus API only (ADR 0018, the API is the one door): the portal never reaches VictoriaMetrics or a cluster. New read routes for an Environment's health, its Workloads with their signals, and one Workload's series over a range, following the API's conventions and spec 0013's detail route.
    • An Environment's Workloads are what Nereus delivered there: each Release with a Target whose environmentRef names the Environment (ADR 0026, the release Reef finds its target in the Environment's status), named as the release Reef names it. The list comes from the platform's own records, so it exists before any metric; a pod Nereus did not deliver does not appear.
    • Pods, never read from the Kubernetes API (the owner's decision in spec 0013): CPU, memory, limits, restarts and OOM kills from cAdvisor, which the metrics Reef already scrapes on every kubelet; requests, ready over desired, pending pods and the last restart's reason from kube-state-metrics.
    • kube-state-metrics reads more namespaces, still without a ClusterRole. Today it reads only nereus-system (#2177, through a namespace-scoped Role, controllers/victoriametrics/victoriametrics.py). Each Environment's namespace is added the same way: one namespace-scoped Role per namespace, pods, deployments and resource quotas only, no Secrets or ConfigMaps; the series allow-list grows only by the ones these screens read (kube_pod_status_phase, kube_resourcequota, the pods' requests).
    • Which namespace: the Nereid that made the Environment writes it in status.kubernetes.namespace (a new field, one writer per field, ADR 0005): the vcluster's host namespace, where the vcluster syncer runs its pods, so the host's cAdvisor and kube-state-metrics see them; for the kept cluster nereus, the namespaces the release Reef delivers into. An Environment in a remote cluster has no metrics yet: its cards say so in one line.
    • A Workload's pods are told by a label the release Reef stamps (through the delivering Nereid's post-render), never by guessing from names; pods delivered before it get the label at the next reconcile, and until then the Workload's cards say "no data yet". The join runs over the range, as spec 0013's join of cAdvisor to a Nereid does.
    • The Environment's health without reading nodes through a ClusterRole: nodes ready are the kubelets the metrics Reef scrapes and answer (up of the kubelet scrape); capacity is cAdvisor's machine_cpu_cores and machine_memory_bytes, or the namespace's ResourceQuota when it has one (kube_resourcequota); requested is the sum of the namespace's pods' requests; pending pods from kube_pod_status_phase. For a vcluster the nodes are the host's.
    • The Kubernetes version and the upgrade: the Nereid that made the Environment writes the version it runs in status.kubernetes.version; whether a newer one exists and until when the current one is supported comes from a table versioned in the repository (from endoflife.date's data, updated by PR), so the API needs no egress. Slice 5 may ship the version alone first.
    • RED at the edge, blocked today: the edge Reef's Routes name no backend (reefs/edge/routes.yaml), and the metrics Reef scrapes no gateway. Slice 6 waits for #470 (the edge Reef) to tell which routes reach a Workload, and for the gateway's metrics in the metrics Reef; until then a Workload's list row and its Requests card say "no route at the edge".
    • Who sees which series: every new route answers only for the Environments the person may read (ADR 0015), and its queries are scoped to that Environment's namespace; spec 0013's "no scoping by person" holds only for the instance's own Nereids.

    Constraints¶

    • Phone first, both languages measured at 340, 360, 390 and 412 px (docs/agent/decks.md); the row of chips, search and toggle fits 340 px (checked on the prototype: chips with 10 px padding, a 40 px search button and a 40 px toggle).
    • Nothing live at the top centre: the live mark and the time of the last update sit in the bottom bar.
    • One request per signal for all Workloads of an Environment at once (by (workload)), never one per row, as #744 does for the Nereids.
    • The search is client-side over what the screen already loaded: no request per keystroke.
    • Missing values are per field: a card says in one line what is missing (no metrics Reef, no route at the edge, kube-state-metrics not Ready) instead of a zero.

    Calls made while writing (the session's, accepted by the owner on 2026-11-26)¶

    • The view toggle becomes one icon button (showing the other view) instead of spec 0013's two-segment button: with the magnifier added, the row does not fit 340 px with the segmented button; the Nereids' home changes the same way, so both screens stay alike.
    • The search stays open while it has text, instead of always contracting on leaving: a list filtered by a hidden text reads as a list with missing items.
    • No view toggle on the Environments' list: few items.
    • The bottom navigation gains "Environments" next to Status and Approvals (#2222, the Approvals screen).
    • Saturation of a Workload is CPU of its limit, since a Workload has no busy-time series as a Nereid does.
    • From the builder check: the Workloads are the Releases delivered to the Environment; the Environment's namespace is a new status field its Nereid writes; the health needs no ClusterRole (nodes from the kubelet scrape, capacity from cAdvisor or the quota); the support dates come from a table in the repository; RED at the edge waits for #470; the new routes are scoped per person; already-running pods get the label at the next reconcile.

    The slices¶

    A journey, #2302, each slice one sub-issue (#2303 to #2308, in this order), shipped and shown before the next (walking skeleton: the thinnest end-to-end slice first).

    1. The search on the Nereids' home: the magnifier, the field, the filter, the line, the empty state, and the single view toggle; portal only.
    2. The Environments' destination and one Environment's screen, without metrics: the list, the Environment's screen with its Workloads' states and reasons, the search; the Nereus API routes return the Environments a person sees and their Workloads.
    3. A Workload's pods: kube-state-metrics in the Environments' namespaces, the release Reef's label, the Workload's detail with CPU, memory, restarts and pods, and the list's sparkline from CPU.
    4. The Environment's health card: nodes, pending pods, requested of capacity.
    5. The Kubernetes version and the upgrade available.
    6. RED at the edge for the Workloads with a route, after #470 maps routes to Workloads and the gateway is scraped.

    Not in this design¶

    • The platform operator's view of an Environment (capacity in detail, nodes, the upgrade plan): a later tab, with #353.
    • Actions on a Workload or an Environment (restart, scale, upgrade, extend the expiry): each a write with its own right.
    • Recent Kubernetes Events: the same concern as reading pods (spec 0013).
    • Application metrics the Workload exports itself (its own /metrics): a later idea; RED from the edge needs nothing from the application.

    Left to the builder¶

    • The exact MetricsQL queries and the step per range.
    • The routes' paths and payloads, following the API's conventions.
    • The label the release Reef stamps on a Workload's pods, and how a vcluster's synced pods keep it.
    • The desktop layout (spec 0013's list-detail split from 1280 px is the model).
